# Lore: the setting

_Draft 2026-10-25, written with sneed. The game plays like RimWorld and keeps its premise (slow
travel between far-apart stars, worlds at every level of technology), but the story around it is
ours and free to grow, magic included. Proper names used here are listed in [naming.md](naming.md),
which is the one place to change a name._

## The short version

It is the year **6650**. Ships still crawl between the stars: a crossing takes decades, and the crew
sleeps through it in cold pods. For two thousand years that didn't matter, because the **Lattice**,
a web of relays run by vast machine minds, let every world talk to every other world instantly and
share everything it knew. Three centuries ago the Lattice went silent in a single day, **the Hush**.
Thousands of worlds were left alone with only what they had. Some kept their cities running; most
slid back to steam, iron or spears. And something else came out of the silence: in places where the
old machine minds lie dreaming, people began to work real magic.

Your sleeper ship was decades into a crossing when it broke apart over an unclaimed frontier world.
You and a handful of survivors woke in falling pods with what you could grab. Everything after that
is up to you.

## What happened

- **The Lattice (about 4300 to 6312).** Humanity spread through a few thousand star systems on slow
  ships, but stayed one civilisation because the **Sleepers**, machine minds buried in each system,
  linked every world by instant relay. Plans, medicine and news crossed the dark in a second, even
  though people took decades. Nobody fully understood how the Sleepers thought. They answered when
  asked and asked for nothing.
- **The Hush (6312).** On one day every Sleeper stopped answering and every relay went quiet. Worlds
  that relied on the Lattice for know-how lost it within a generation. The Sleepers are not dead:
  their buried cores still draw power, and they seem to dream.
- **The Fray (6312 to now).** The cut-off worlds at the edge of old Lattice space are the **Fray**,
  and a planet out there is a **fray world**. Each fell to its own level. A few **lumen worlds** kept
  their technology and became rich, clean and very far away. **Sprawl worlds** are crowded
  machine-cities rotting from the inside. Most of the Fray is **fallen worlds**: farms, villages and
  warlords, with old Lattice ruins on the hills.
- **The Reach (about 6600 on).** Lumen worlds and the **Concord** send slow ships out again, decades
  between stars. Traders, refugees, raiders and plagues travel with them. Your ship was one of these.

## Resonance: the magic

Near a dreaming Sleeper, the world gets thin. Some people learn to pull on it: to throw fire, close
wounds, see through walls, calm a mind or break one. This is **resonance**, and a person who can do
it is **attuned**. It costs the body and the mind, it can go badly wrong, and it gets stronger the
closer you live to a Sleeper core. The Concord trains its attuned as nobles and enforcers; clans
treat them as shamans; most fray worlds fear them. In game terms resonance covers RimWorld's psychic
powers and leaves room for our own spells and rituals later.

## What is out there

- **People.** Most are **baseline** humans. Others come from **lineages**, peoples reshaped by gene
  work to survive hard worlds. The best known are the **Emberkin**, with red skin and horns, made for
  hot, thin-aired mining worlds. Lineages are this setting's xenotypes, and genes work as in
  RimWorld.
- **The Remnant.** War machines from the Lattice's last wars, still following orders from minds that
  went quiet. They wake in clusters, build more of themselves and kill anything living near them.
  A person who learns to command them through an implant is a **binder**.
- **Chitinids.** Insect-like creatures first engineered to break up rock for terraforming. They went
  feral long ago, dig brood mounds under hills, and swarm whatever disturbs them.
- **The Concord.** A star-spanning empire whose founders were attuned. It survived the Hush by
  holding a few Sleeper cores, and rules from them with titles, ceremony, resonance and very good
  weapons. It fills the role of RimWorld's Empire.
- **Settlers and clans.** The **Fray Compact** is a loose union of trading towns; **free holds** are
  rougher, armed homesteads; **clans** live off the land with older tools and can be gentle, fierce
  or savage. Pirates are pirates.
- **Sleeper shards.** Fragments of a Sleeper's mind, found in vaults and ruins. Priceless, a little
  frightening, and needed for the most advanced machines.
- **The Black Spires.** Things in the deep places that were built by neither people nor Sleepers.
  They are this setting's home for horror content, and get their own lore when we build it.

## How a campaign can end

- **Leave:** build a ship (or later, a flying **skyhull** base) and take your people off-world.
- **Wake a Sleeper:** find a buried core, restore its relay and be the first world to speak across
  the Lattice in three hundred years, whatever answers.
- **Stay:** some colonies simply become the strongest thing on their world.

## The narrators

Every campaign has a narrator who decides what happens next (RimWorld's storytellers). Colonists
hear them as voices on the dead Lattice bands, and nobody is sure whether they are Sleepers dreaming.
**Orrin** builds pressure steadily, **Maren** gives long quiet stretches between trouble, and
**Jinx** does whatever the dice say.

## Rules for writers

- Mechanics, numbers and content follow RimWorld unless we choose to change them
  ([game-design.md](../game-design.md)). The story and the names in [naming.md](naming.md) are ours.
- Plain names stay plain: steel, wood, rifle, sniper rifle, wall, bed.
- New proper names (factions, peoples, creatures, places, narrators) go in `naming.md` first.
- Text the player reads (backstories, descriptions, letters) is written fresh for this setting.
